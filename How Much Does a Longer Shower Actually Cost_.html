How much does a longer shower actually cost?

A 20-minute shower is not "bad". It is four times a 5-minute shower in water, heating energy and money, because every minute runs the same flow. This page calculates what that means in gallons, kWh and dollars, and shows what changes when the showerhead or water heater changes.

Base case: 2.5 gpm standard showerhead, electric resistance water heater, water heated from 50°F to 120°F (a 70°F rise), U.S. average prices.

Shorter shower or lower-flow showerhead?

Cost per shower with an electric heater. Cutting flow from 2.5 to 2.0 gpm saves 20% on every shower, the same as shaving 1 minute off a 5-minute shower or 4 minutes off a 20-minute one. For a long shower, the shower length changes the answer far more than the showerhead.

Notes and how AI was used

  • Not Rochester data. Prices are U.S. averages (EPA 2024 water + wastewater; EIA electricity for July 2026). A Rochester-area utility tariff would be a better local input.
  • Heating is probably overstated. The model heats every gallon by 70°F, but real showers mix hot and cold water to roughly 105°F, which would cut heating energy by about 21%.
  • Real showers are shorter. A secondary source (The Shower Head Store) reports an average of 8.2 minutes at 2.1 gpm, about 17 gallons; I did not verify it against a primary study.
  • Water price is an average. Many utilities have fixed base charges and tiered rates, so the true cost of one shower can differ.
  • AI use: AI was used for the formulas, calculations and page design.
